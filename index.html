05 / The process

Test, report, fix

  1. 01

    Agree the scope

    We discuss your systems and concerns, agree the scope, testing windows and escalation contacts, and obtain written authorisation before testing begins.

  2. 02

    Test and verify

    We run vulnerability assessments and manual penetration tests, plus controlled phishing simulations and staff awareness training where agreed. We verify findings before reporting them.

  3. 03

    Explain and prioritise

    We walk you through the report, explain the business impact of each finding and agree a prioritised plan, so you know what needs attention first.

  4. 04

    Implement and retest

    Our team can implement the recommendations or work alongside your existing IT provider. A free retest within 90 days checks that the fixes address the findings.

Typical timeline: A full audit takes around 5-6 weeks from kick-off to final report. We’ll confirm the schedule when we agree on the scope.

06 / FAQs

Before you book an audit

We already have IT support. Isn’t that enough?

Check what your contract includes. Day-to-day IT support may not cover penetration testing, phishing simulations or data protection gap analysis. We can work alongside your existing provider where further assessment or remediation is needed.

What does it cost?

Cost depends on what needs to be assessed. The free consultation helps us understand your requirements so we can propose a scope and provide a quote.

Will testing disrupt our business?

We agree on testing windows, scope and escalation contacts before work begins, so the work can be planned around your operations.

Can you help with customer or insurer security reviews?

We can help you respond to security questionnaires, identify gaps in your supporting evidence and document the work needed to address them.

How will you handle our data?

Audit data is held encrypted in the EU and deleted on an agreed schedule after report acceptance.

Does GDPR require us to have a cybersecurity audit?

GDPR doesn’t require every organisation to commission a cybersecurity audit. It does require appropriate security measures and checks that they work. An audit helps you assess risks and document your findings.

GDPR, Article 32

We supply an organisation covered by NIS2. What do we need?

A customer covered by NIS2 may require security controls, incident reporting commitments or audit rights in your contract. We review those requirements, identify gaps and help you prepare supporting documentation.

What if our site is already compromised?

Call us for incident response, including malware removal, backdoor patching, system restoration and forensic review. We also help assess reporting obligations. Reportable personal data breaches require DPC notification without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.
